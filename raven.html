The RAVEN

NASA's Research Aircraft for eVTOL Enabling techNologies

Content on this page is adapted from NASA's official RAVEN program description. The images on this page belong to NASA.
Source: NASA, "Research Aircraft for eVTOL Enabling techNologies (RAVEN)" (view for more information).

The RAVEN project is a collaborative effort with Georgia Tech meant to enable researchers and industries to conduct research, develop practical methods for vehicle design and operation, and train future engineers.

The RAVEN is currently in a small-scale prototype phase, called the SWFT, for development. CAD models of this version are available, and the device uses off-the-shelf parts so that it can be assembled easily. Though the full-scale version has not been made yet, RAVEN SWFT is actively being used for research and development.

    The RAVEN...
  • Provides open flight data available to the public
  • Allows research payloads (such as avionics, sensors, and flight computers) through adequate size
  • Presents configuration representative of industry eVTOLs, with relevant challenges in aerodynamics, propulsion integration, acoustics, and flight control
  • Addresses “real world” issues in eVTOL design and development for Advanced Air Mobility (AAM) through relevant scale.
  • Presents a configuration representative of industry eVTOLs, with relevant challenges in aerodynamics, propulsion integration, acoustics, and flight control.
  • Serves as more than just a flight demonstrator: a “flying laboratory” for enduring research and technology development.
